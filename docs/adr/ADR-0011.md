# ADR-0011: Umbral layer

## Context
A central challenge in formalizing exact identities on a lattice is that continuous differential operators ($\partial_x$) and integrals ($\int$) do not exist exactly on discrete spaces. Standard approximations involve limits ($\alpha \to 0$), which invoke heavy real analysis and Measure Theory, violating our core rule of purely algebraic formalization. We must replace differential identities (like $[\partial_x, x] = 1$) with exact discrete analogs. 

## Options
1. **Real analysis limits:** Formalize standard lattice spacing limits (forbidden by Phase 0 rules).
2. **Matrix representations:** Attempt to define operators on finite-dimensional matrices.
3. **Umbral Calculus:** Use exact finite-difference operators and falling factorials on rings of functions and polynomials.

## Decision
We will construct an **umbral calculus layer**. We will define functions on `ℤ → R` and `Site L → R` (where `R` is a commutative ring) for the shift operator $E$ and the finite difference operator $\Delta = E - 1$. The umbral map will be defined on polynomials to map standard powers $X^n$ to falling factorials (Pochhammer symbols, $x^{\underline{n}}$). Crucially, the Heisenberg pair will be strictly defined on `ℤ → R` and polynomials, not on finite-dimensional matrices. 

## Attacks
1. **Why not define $\Delta$ over finite-dimensional matrices?**
   *Response:* The trace argument mathematically prohibits exact finite-dimensional representations of the commutation relation $[A, B] = 1$ in characteristic zero. This mathematical fact is precisely *why* bosonic CCR only holds on infinite-dimensional polynomial modules or bounded budget windows, rather than globally on finite lattice matrices.
2. **Will this work for functions on `Site L` (which is `ZMod L`)?**
   *Response:* The Heisenberg pair cannot act faithfully on `ZMod L`, but difference operators $\Delta$ and shift $E$ act perfectly well on periodic functions, providing exact discrete Leibniz rules and telescoping summation without limits.
3. **Newton expansions instead of Taylor series might diverge?**
   *Response:* We only apply Newton expansions on finite domains (budget windows) or formal polynomials, meaning sums are strictly finite. No convergence or topological limits are needed.

## Rollback
If the abstract `ℤ → R` formulation becomes too cumbersome for algebraic manipulation, we can strictly limit the umbral layer to `Polynomial R` where $\Delta P(X) = P(X+1) - P(X)$ is natively supported and bounded by degree.

## Consequences for Lean
*   We will define the shift $E$ and $\Delta$ explicitly as `Module.End` or `LinearMap`.
*   We rely on exact finite sum telescoping (`Finset.sum_range_succ_sub_sum`).
*   **Mathlib audit needed:** Check for `fwdDiff`, `fwdDiff_iter_eq_sum_shift`, `descPochhammer`, and related `Polynomial` lemmas.
