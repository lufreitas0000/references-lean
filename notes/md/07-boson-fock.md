# Umbral Boson Fock Layer: Algebraic Foundations

This document outlines the rigorous mathematical formulation of the bosonic layer for a 1D lattice quantum field theory, specifically tailored for formalization in Lean 4. To sidestep the formidable analytic subtleties of infinite-dimensional topological vector spaces—such as domain issues of unbounded operators and infinite-dimensional trace paradoxes—we employ an **umbral (polynomial) approach**. 

In this framework, the Fock space is realized purely algebraically, and creation/annihilation operators are globally defined endomorphisms.

---

## 1. The Carrier Space and Basic Operators

Let $Q$ denote the index set of bosonic modes (e.g., momentum modes on a 1D lattice, formally `BosonIdx Q`). 

We define the **carrier space** (the algebraic Fock space) $\mathcal{F}$ as the commutative ring of multivariate polynomials over $\mathbb{C}$ generated by the variables $\{X_m\}_{m \in Q}$. In the Lean 4 formalization, this corresponds directly to:
$$ \mathcal{F} := \texttt{MvPolynomial Q } \mathbb{C} $$

Since $\mathcal{F}$ is a $\mathbb{C}$-vector space, quantum mechanical observables and operations are formalized as linear endomorphisms, residing in $\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{F})$ (formally `Module.End ℂ MvPolynomial`).

### Creation and Annihilation Operators
We define the **creation operator** $a^\dagger_m$ as the operator of left-multiplication by the formal variable $X_m$:
$$ a^\dagger_m(p) := X_m \cdot p \quad \forall p \in \mathcal{F} $$

We define the **annihilation operator** $a_m$ as the formal partial derivative with respect to $X_m$ (corresponding to `MvPolynomial.pderiv`):
$$ a_m(p) := \frac{\partial}{\partial X_m} p \quad \forall p \in \mathcal{F} $$

### Unnormalized Currents
In 1D lattice models (such as Luttinger liquids or conformal field theories), one frequently encounters unnormalized currents. Assuming each mode $m \in Q$ has an associated non-zero weight (which we will denote by $m$ itself for notational convenience), we define the current operators as:
$$ J^\dagger_m := X_m \cdot \quad \text{and} \quad J_m := m \frac{\partial}{\partial X_m} $$
Note that $J^\dagger_m = a^\dagger_m$, whereas $J_m = m \, a_m$.

## 2. Canonical Commutation Relations and the Vacuum

By mapping the Heisenberg algebra to the Weyl algebra of derivations on a polynomial ring, we completely eliminate analytic domain issues. The operators $a_m$ and $a^\dagger_m$ are everywhere-defined, bounded (in the discrete topology of the formal algebraic structure), and their commutators close exactly in $\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{F})$.

### The Exact CCR
For any $m, m' \in Q$, the **Canonical Commutation Relations (CCR)** hold as exact algebraic identities in $\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{F})$:
$$ [a_m, a^\dagger_{m'}] = \delta_{mm'} \mathbb{I} $$
$$ [a_m, a_{m'}] = 0 $$
$$ [a^\dagger_m, a^\dagger_{m'}] = 0 $$
*Proof sketch for formalization:* The commutator $[a_m, a^\dagger_{m'}]p = \partial_m(X_{m'} p) - X_{m'}(\partial_m p)$. By the formal Leibniz rule on `MvPolynomial`, this evaluates to $(\partial_m X_{m'})p + X_{m'}\partial_m p - X_{m'}\partial_m p = \delta_{mm'} p$.

This provides a rigorous, paradox-free realization of the umbral Heisenberg pair.

### The Vacuum Functional
The vacuum state $|0\rangle$ corresponds to the polynomial $1 \in \mathcal{F}$. We define the **vacuum functional** $\omega_0 : \mathcal{F} \to \mathbb{C}$ as the operator that extracts the constant coefficient of the polynomial (corresponding to `constantCoeff` in `MvPolynomial`). 

In the language of algebraic QFT, $\omega_0(p) \equiv \langle 0 | p | 0 \rangle$. The functional satisfies the following key rules:
1. **Normalization:** $\omega_0(1) = 1$.
2. **Annihilation condition:** For any polynomial $p \in \mathcal{F}$, the evaluation of $\omega_0(a_m p)$ extracts the linear coefficient of $X_m$ in $p$. Consequently, if $p$ has no linear dependence on $X_m$, $\omega_0(a_m p) = 0$. 
3. **Current vacuum annihilation:** $\omega_0(a_m) = 0$, reflecting the physical intuition that $a_m |0\rangle = 0$.

## 3. Number and Energy Operators

We define macroscopic observables by lifting operations to sums over modes. 

### The Number Operator
The total **number operator** $N \in \mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{F})$ is defined via the formal Euler operator:
$$ N := \sum_{m \in Q} X_m \frac{\partial}{\partial X_m} = \sum_{m \in Q} a^\dagger_m a_m $$
Because $N$ is the standard degree operator on polynomials, any monomial $p = \prod_{m} X_m^{r_m}$ is an eigenvector of $N$:
$$ N \left( \prod_{m} X_m^{r_m} \right) = \left( \sum_{m} r_m \right) \prod_{m} X_m^{r_m} $$

### The Energy (Hamiltonian) Operator
Assuming a linear dispersion relation where the energy of mode $m$ is proportional to $m$, the **energy operator** $H \in \mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{F})$ is defined as:
$$ H := \sum_{m \in Q} m X_m \frac{\partial}{\partial X_m} = \sum_{m \in Q} J^\dagger_m a_m $$
The operator $H$ measures the *weighted degree* of the polynomial. Monomials are also strict eigenvectors of $H$:
$$ H \left( \prod_{m} X_m^{r_m} \right) = \left( \sum_{m} m \cdot r_m \right) \prod_{m} X_m^{r_m} $$

## 4. The Nilpotent Truncated Exponential

A standard difficulty in formalizing QFT is defining exponential operators, such as shift operators or Bogoliubov transformations, since the formal power series $\exp(A) = \sum \frac{1}{n!} A^n$ requires topological completions and convergence proofs. 

In the umbral approach, we bypass this entirely for annihilation currents by utilizing a weight filtration on $\mathcal{F}$.

### Weight Subspaces and Nilpotency
Let $\mathcal{F}_{\leq K}$ be the finite-dimensional subspace of $\mathcal{F}$ consisting of polynomials whose weighted degree (energy) is bounded by $K$:
$$ \mathcal{F}_{\leq K} := \mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{C}} \left\{ \prod_{m} X_m^{r_m} \mathrel{\Bigg|} \sum_{m} m \cdot r_m \leq K \right\} $$

Consider an arbitrary linear combination of annihilation currents, $J(\lambda) = \sum_m \lambda_m J_m$. Because each derivative operator $J_m = m \partial_m$ strictly lowers the weighted degree of any monomial by at least $m \geq 1$, the operator $J(\lambda)$ acts as a strictly lowering operator on the weight filtration.

Therefore, applying $J(\lambda)$ exactly $K+1$ times to any polynomial in $\mathcal{F}_{\leq K}$ must annihilate it. As an identity restricted to this subspace:
$$ \left( \sum_m \lambda_m J_m \right)^{K+1} \Big|_{\mathcal{F}_{\leq K}} = 0 $$

### The Truncated Exponential
Thanks to this exact nilpotency, the exponential of the annihilation current on the subspace $\mathcal{F}_{\leq K}$ truncates to a finite polynomial in the operator algebra. We define the **truncated exponential** operator:
$$ \exp_K(J(\lambda)) := \sum_{n=0}^K \frac{1}{n!} \left( \sum_m \lambda_m J_m \right)^n $$

**Key Theorem:** On the subspace $\mathcal{F}_{\leq K}$, $\exp_K(J(\lambda))$ is an *honest, well-defined exponential*. That is, there is no truncation error, no need for limits, and no topological completion required. It acts exactly as the full analytic exponential would, making it perfectly suited for rigorous constructive physics in Lean 4.
