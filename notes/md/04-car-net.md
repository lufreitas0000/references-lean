# Chapters 1.4 & 1.5: CAR Representation and the Lattice AQFT Net

This document rigorously constructs the Canonical Anticommutation Relation (CAR) representation on a fermionic Fock space and utilizes it to define the lattice Algebraic Quantum Field Theory (AQFT) net. The mathematical formalism presented here bridges abstract operator algebras with discrete lattice models, preparing the ground for the study of quantum spin chains and fermionic systems.

---

## 1.4 The CAR Representation and Fock Space

To describe non-interacting and interacting fermions, we first require a rigorous formulation of the CAR algebra and its fundamental representations.

**Definition 1.4.1 (CAR Representation).** Let $\iota$ be a finite, totally ordered index set, and let $V$ be a complex Hilbert space. A CAR representation, denoted formally as $\mathtt{CARRep}(\iota, V)$, consists of a pair of mappings $c, c^\dagger : \iota \to \mathrm{End}(V)$ satisfying the Canonical Anticommutation Relations:
$$ \{c_i, c_j\} = 0, \quad \{c_i^\dagger, c_j^\dagger\} = 0, \quad \{c_i, c_j^\dagger\} = \delta_{ij} \mathbb{I}, \quad \forall i, j \in \iota $$
where $\{A, B\} := AB + BA$ is the anticommutator, and $\mathbb{I}$ is the identity operator on $V$.

To instantiate this representation, we construct the concrete finite-dimensional fermionic Fock space.

**Definition 1.4.2 (Concrete Fock Space).** Let $\mathrm{Finset}(\iota)$ denote the power set of $\iota$ (since $\iota$ is finite, this is the set of all subsets). The concrete Fock space is defined as the space of complex-valued functions on these finite sets:
$$ \mathtt{Fock}(\iota) := \mathrm{Finset}(\iota) \to \mathbb{C} $$
We equip $\mathtt{Fock}(\iota)$ with the standard occupation number basis $\{ |S\rangle \}_{S \subseteq \iota}$, where $|S\rangle(S') = \delta_{S, S'}$.

**Theorem 1.4.3 (Dimension of Fock Space).** As a module over $\mathbb{C}$, the dimension (or `Module.finrank`) of the Fock space is given by:
$$ \dim_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathtt{Fock}(\iota)) = 2^{|\iota|} $$
*Proof.* The basis elements are in bijection with the elements of $\mathrm{Finset}(\iota)$. The cardinality of the power set of $\iota$ is $2^{|\iota|}$. $\blacksquare$

### 1.4.1 Creation and Annihilation Operators
We define the action of the creation and annihilation operators on the basis states $|S\rangle$ by incorporating a Jordan-Wigner-esque sign factor to properly encode the anticommuting nature of fermions.

Let $\sigma(i, S)$ count the number of occupied states strictly preceding $i$ in the total order of $\iota$:
$$ \sigma(i, S) := \#\{k \in S \mid k < i\} $$

We define the operators $c, c^\dagger$ as follows:
$$
c_i^\dagger |S\rangle = 
\begin{cases} 
0 & \text{if } i \in S \\
(-1)^{\sigma(i, S)} |S \cup \{i\}\rangle & \text{if } i \notin S 
\end{cases}
$$
$$
c_i |S\rangle = 
\begin{cases} 
(-1)^{\sigma(i, S)} |S \setminus \{i\}\rangle & \text{if } i \in S \\
0 & \text{if } i \notin S 
\end{cases}
$$

**Lemma 1.4.4 (Inner Product and Adjoints).** We define a natural inner product on $\mathtt{Fock}(\iota)$ by declaring the basis $\{ |S\rangle \}$ to be orthonormal:
$$ \langle S | S' \rangle = \delta_{S, S'} $$
Under this inner product, $c_i^\dagger = (c_i)^\dagger$.

*Proof.* By direct computation of matrix elements: $\langle S \cup \{i\} | c_i^\dagger | S \rangle = (-1)^{\sigma(i, S)}$ for $i \notin S$. Conversely, $\langle S | c_i | S \cup \{i\} \rangle = (-1)^{\sigma(i, S \cup \{i\})}$. Since $i \notin S$, the elements less than $i$ in $S$ and $S \cup \{i\}$ are identical, hence $\sigma(i, S) = \sigma(i, S \cup \{i\})$. Thus, the matrix elements coincide, and $c_i^\dagger$ is the adjoint of $c_i$. $\blacksquare$

### 1.4.2 Chiralities and Lattices
In typical models of 1+1D quantum field theory (e.g., massless Dirac or Thirring models), the index set carries internal degrees of freedom. We frequently instantiate $\iota$ to encode *chirality* and *spatial modes*:
$$ \iota = \mathrm{Chirality} \times \mathrm{Mode}_L $$
where $\mathrm{Chirality} \in \{R, L\}$ (Right-moving, Left-moving) and $\mathrm{Mode}_L = \mathbb{Z}_L$ is a periodic lattice of size $L$. The ordering on $\iota$ is given by the lexicographical order on the product space.

## 1.5 The Lattice AQFT Net

In the algebraic approach to quantum field theory (AQFT), the primary object of study is the *net of local observable algebras* mapped to spatial regions.

Let $\mathcal{L} = \mathbb{Z}_L$ denote the set of lattice sites. We consider the index set $\iota = \mathcal{L}$ (for a single species of fermion). We construct the local algebras over the Hilbert space $\mathcal{H} = \mathtt{Fock}(\mathcal{L})$.

**Definition 1.5.1 (Local Algebras).** For any spatial region $I \subseteq \mathcal{L}$, we define the local algebra $\mathfrak{A}(I)$ as the unital $\mathtt{StarSubalgebra}$ of $\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{H})$ generated by the local creation and annihilation operators:
$$ \mathfrak{A}(I) := \mathrm{Gen}_{\mathbb{C}}\{ c_j, c_j^\dagger \mid j \in I \} \subseteq \mathrm{End}(\mathtt{Fock}(\mathcal{L})) $$

The assignment $I \mapsto \mathfrak{A}(I)$ satisfies the foundational axioms of a Lattice AQFT net.

### 1.5.2 AQFT Axioms on the Lattice

**Theorem 1.5.2 (Isotony).** For any two subsets $I, J \subseteq \mathcal{L}$,
$$ I \subseteq J \implies \mathfrak{A}(I) \subseteq \mathfrak{A}(J) $$
*Proof.* If $I \subseteq J$, then the generating set of $\mathfrak{A}(I)$ is a subset of the generating set of $\mathfrak{A}(J)$. Since both are closed under the *-algebra operations, the inclusion of subalgebras immediately follows. $\blacksquare$

**Definition 1.5.3 (Parity Automorphism and Grading).** Let $\Gamma = \prod_{j \in \mathcal{L}} (1 - 2c_j^\dagger c_j)$ be the global parity operator (equivalent to $(-1)^{\hat{N}}$ where $\hat{N}$ is the total number operator). We define the parity automorphism $\alpha \in \mathrm{Aut}(\mathfrak{A}(\mathcal{L}))$ by:
$$ \alpha(A) := \Gamma A \Gamma^\dagger $$
Acting on the generators, $\alpha(c_j) = -c_j$ and $\alpha(c_j^\dagger) = -c_j^\dagger$. This automorphism induces a $\mathbb{Z}_2$-grading on the algebras:
$$ \mathfrak{A}(I) = \mathfrak{A}_+(I) \oplus \mathfrak{A}_-(I) $$
where $\mathfrak{A}_\pm(I) := \{ A \in \mathfrak{A}(I) \mid \alpha(A) = \pm A \}$. Elements of $\mathfrak{A}_+$ are called *even* (bosonic), and elements of $\mathfrak{A}_-$ are called *odd* (fermionic).

**Theorem 1.5.4 (Twisted Locality).** Let $I$ and $J$ be disjoint regions, $I \cap J = \emptyset$. For homogeneous elements $A \in \mathfrak{A}_{\sigma_A}(I)$ and $B \in \mathfrak{A}_{\sigma_B}(J)$ (where $\sigma \in \{0, 1\}$ corresponds to even/odd parity), we have:
$$ AB = (-1)^{\sigma_A \sigma_B} BA $$
*Corollary:* The even subnet, $I \mapsto \mathfrak{A}_+(I)$, is *strictly local*, meaning $[\mathfrak{A}_+(I), \mathfrak{A}_+(J)] = 0$ for $I \cap J = \emptyset$.

*Proof.* By the CAR algebra, any pair of single operators $c_i^{(\dagger)}$ and $c_j^{(\dagger)}$ for $i \in I, j \in J$ ($i \neq j$) exactly anticommute. Any element $A \in \mathfrak{A}_{\sigma_A}(I)$ is a linear combination of products of length $n_A \equiv \sigma_A \pmod 2$. Moving $n_A$ local operators past $n_B$ local operators yields a sign of $(-1)^{n_A n_B} = (-1)^{\sigma_A \sigma_B}$. $\blacksquare$

**Theorem 1.5.5 (Additivity).** For any subsets $I, J \subseteq \mathcal{L}$,
$$ \mathfrak{A}(I \cup J) = \mathfrak{A}(I) \vee \mathfrak{A}(J) $$
where $\vee$ denotes the generated von Neumann algebra (or $\mathtt{StarSubalgebra}$ generation in the finite-dimensional case).
*Proof.* The generators of $\mathfrak{A}(I \cup J)$ are precisely $\{c_k, c_k^\dagger \mid k \in I \cup J\}$. This is exactly the union of the generators of $\mathfrak{A}(I)$ and $\mathfrak{A}(J)$, which naturally generate $\mathfrak{A}(I) \vee \mathfrak{A}(J)$. $\blacksquare$

### 1.5.3 Global Algebra and Kinematics

**Theorem 1.5.6 (Irreducibility and Size).** The global algebra evaluated on the entire lattice, $\mathfrak{A}(\mathcal{L})$, is irreducible on the Fock space and is isomorphic to the full endomorphism algebra:
$$ \mathfrak{A}(\mathcal{L}) \cong \mathrm{End}(\mathtt{Fock}(\mathcal{L})) $$
Consequently, the complex dimension of the global algebra is:
$$ \dim_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathfrak{A}(\mathcal{L})) = 4^L $$
*(Note: For a generalized index set $\iota$, the dimension is $4^{|\iota|}$. In the model where $\iota = \mathrm{Chirality} \times \mathcal{L}$, the dimension expands to $4^{2L} = 16^L$.)*

*Proof.* The Fock space provides a faithful, irreducible representation of the CAR algebra. By Burnside's Theorem for finite-dimensional operator algebras over $\mathbb{C}$, an irreducible subalgebra of $\mathrm{End}(V)$ is the whole of $\mathrm{End}(V)$. Since $\dim(\mathtt{Fock}(\mathcal{L})) = 2^L$, its endomorphism algebra has dimension $(2^L)^2 = 4^L$. $\blacksquare$

**Theorem 1.5.7 (Translation Covariance).** Let the spatial translation on the lattice be given by the shift operator modulo $L$: $T(j) = j + 1$. This discrete translation induces a unitary operator $U_T$ on $\mathtt{Fock}(\mathcal{L})$. We define a global *-automorphism $\tau \in \mathrm{Aut}(\mathfrak{A}(\mathcal{L}))$:
$$ \tau(A) := U_T A U_T^\dagger $$
The net is translationally covariant, meaning:
$$ \tau(\mathfrak{A}(I)) = \mathfrak{A}(I + 1) \quad \forall I \subseteq \mathcal{L} $$

### 1.5.4 Lattice Haag Duality

A critical structural property of AQFT nets is Haag Duality, connecting causal complements to commutants. Due to the fermionic nature of our net, the strict commutant is modified to a *twisted* commutant.

**Definition 1.5.8 (Twisted Commutant).** The twisted commutant of a local algebra $\mathfrak{A}(I)$, denoted $\mathfrak{A}(I)^{Z'}$, is defined using the graded commutator $[X, A]_Z$:
$$ \mathfrak{A}(I)^{Z'} := \{ X \in \mathfrak{A}(\mathcal{L}) \mid [X, A]_Z = 0, \ \forall A \in \mathfrak{A}(I) \} $$
where for homogeneous elements $[X, A]_Z = XA - (-1)^{\sigma_X \sigma_A} AX$. Alternatively, $X \in \mathfrak{A}(I)^{Z'} \iff X_+ \in \mathfrak{A}(I)'_+ \text{ and } X_- \in \mathfrak{A}(I)'_-$, where $(\cdot)'$ is the standard commutant.

**Theorem 1.5.9 (Lattice Haag Duality).** For any region $I \subseteq \mathcal{L}$, the lattice AQFT net satisfies Twisted Haag Duality:
$$ \mathfrak{A}(I)^{Z'} = \mathfrak{A}(I^c) $$
where $I^c = \mathcal{L} \setminus I$ is the spatial complement of $I$ on the lattice.
*Proof sketch.* By twisted locality, $\mathfrak{A}(I^c) \subseteq \mathfrak{A}(I)^{Z'}$. Due to the factorization of the full algebra $\mathrm{End}(\mathtt{Fock}(\mathcal{L})) \cong \mathfrak{A}(I) \hat{\otimes} \mathfrak{A}(I^c)$ (where $\hat{\otimes}$ is the graded tensor product) and dimension counting, the inclusion is upgraded to a strict equality.
